Why sea ice matters around Iceland

Iceland sits at the boundary between warm Atlantic water and cold, fresh Polar water. The East Greenland Current carries Polar water and, in some years, drift ice southward through the Denmark Strait, and the north Icelandic shelf can be affected in cold years. Sea ice changes the ocean in several ways:

  • Insulation & albedo — ice cover cuts the exchange of heat between ocean and atmosphere and reflects incoming sunlight.
  • Freshwater and salt — melting adds freshwater and stratifies the surface layer; freezing rejects brine and can drive convection.
  • Momentum — ice drift transfers wind stress to the ocean differently than open water does.
  • Ecosystems & fisheries — ice-edge position and surface stratification affect the timing of the spring bloom and, in turn, the marine food web that HAFRO monitors.

What is being developed

The current Iceland ROMS configuration is ocean-only, so sea ice and its effects have to be prescribed or ignored. We are coupling ROMS to the CICE sea-ice model, which represents ice thickness distribution, drift (with elastic–viscous–plastic rheology), growth and melt, so that the ocean and ice evolve together on the same 2 km grid.

How the coupling works

At every coupling step the two models exchange the fields below (schematic — details may change as development proceeds).

ROMS (ocean) → CICE (ice)CICE (ice) → ROMS (ocean)
Sea surface temperature and salinityHeat flux through the ice/ocean interface
Surface currentsFreshwater flux (melt, freeze, brine rejection)
Sea surface slopeIce–ocean stress (momentum)
Freezing-point heat deficitIce-modified shortwave radiation

Atmospheric forcing (winds, air temperature, humidity, radiation, precipitation) drives both components.

How it will be evaluated

Sea-ice results will be compared against independent observations in the same way as the ocean-only model is compared here against Argo floats, moorings and satellites:

  • Ice concentration and extent from satellite passive-microwave products (for example EUMETSAT OSI SAF and NSIDC).
  • Ice thickness from satellite altimetry and SMOS where available.
  • Hydrography in ice-influenced areas from Argo floats and the Denmark Strait and shelf moorings.
  • Skill relative to the ocean-only ROMS run, to show what sea ice adds.
